## Qualifications and unresolved evidence

1. **No full-kernel assurance:** This tests an independent synthetic witness. It does not exercise the actual Kernel-0 realization, CPU/resource enforcement, concurrent execution, recovery, external effects, or the model-adequacy question.
2. **Timing is not a framework benchmark:** Each measurement includes process startup and benefits from a warmed build. The frameworks were not tested across comparable randomized repetitions. Proptest's logs explicitly say `FileFailurePersistence::SourceParallel` and show saving an example in `tests/proptest_compare.proptest-regressions`, so later arms may replay failures; Hegel also has its own execution/replay behavior. The clocks and caches are uncontrolled. Do not infer superiority from 58 vs 75 ms.
3. **Shrinking tie is unsurprising:** All three violations have the same two-operation minimal witness; this corpus is too easy to differentiate shrinking quality.
4. **Generated spaces not identical across engines:** The same abstract operation alphabet, maximum sequence length and case budget are configured, but the generators and RNGs need not select identical samples or shrink histories. The two harnesses share a source-model calculation, so correlated oracle errors remain possible.
5. **Status:** One simple controlled comparison passes. No framework adoption decision, no production modification, and no claim of universal bug absence are justified.

## Decision

**No adoption change.** Keep proptest as the reference baseline and Hegel as a promising experimental comparator. If the research question warrants a follow-up, design harder, source-derived state histories with independent checking, isolate failure databases and processes per arm, and run multiple seeded repeats. Do not broaden implementation without a new hypothesis and explicit scope.
