# P4 — Perturbation and stateful admission results

**Outcome: PASS within the bounded in-memory prototype scope.** Both conventional Rust `Mutex` and Haskell `MVar` implementations preserved capacity, spending, paid-opt-in and idempotency constraints under normal and intentionally delayed concurrent workers. Their deliberately stale-snapshot modes generated exactly the anticipated competing-operation violations. A single-core, short CPU-contention treatment was **measured as effective** and did not violate the tested business invariants.

**Not a production resource allocator.** No durable journal, process crash recovery, real billing settlement, external provider, network failure, disk outage, state-reconciliation protocol or distributed transaction was tested.

## Actual CI execution

- [GitLab pipeline 3015717109](https://gitlab.com/rock-solid-sites-group/rock-solid-sites-project/-/pipelines/3015717109), job `17650244998`: **SUCCESS**, 65.219 seconds on GitLab-hosted shared AMD64 Linux runner.
- The P4 Python harness itself reported **2.215 seconds**, excluding GitLab setup and initial native compilation.
- **120 repeat trials** completed: four scenarios × five repetitions × two languages × three treatments.
- **T0 normal:** 40 safe trials (4 cases × 5 repeats × 2 languages), all invariants preserved.
- **T1 scheduling pause:** 40 safe trials with a requested 3,000 microsecond sleep inside the critical section; each reported an actually measured pause of at least 2,500 microseconds. Invariants preserved and permitted degradation was increased latency only.
- **T2 known fault:** 40 runs using a pre-barrier stale snapshot. Of these, the 30 capacity / spend-cap / duplicate-ID tests produced the intended wrong acceptance; the 10 authorization cases continued to reject unapproved spending as expected. The safety control therefore distinguished the deliberately broken operation ordering from safe atomic commits.
- Four cases: `capacity-race`, `spend-race`, `authorization`, `duplicate-id`. Every trial reached the start barrier with both workers ready, and the harness independently checked accepted/rejected conservation, committed-ID uniqueness, reserved capacity, charged cents and specific expected counts. Winners are scheduling-dependent but cardinalities are fixed.
- Instrumentation, timeout, compiler or environment errors were classified separately in the harness. No business-invariant RED was observed in the two safe implementations.

### Bounded CPU contention

Using Linux task affinity, the harness selected one of the two CPUs available to this runner, bound the two test executables and a bounded spinner to that CPU, and allowed the spinner for only approximately **1.2 seconds**. Actual spinner process CPU usage was **1,188.0 ms** during **1,200.0 ms** of wall time, demonstrating load was actually generated. The 3 ms critical-section delay and a matching baseline were retained.

| Same-core measurement | Baseline median | With contention median | Observed ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rust, spend-race | 7.688 ms | 12.964 ms | 1.686× |
| Haskell, spend-race | 21.832 ms | 27.950 ms | 1.280× |

Five trials per condition were taken for each language, and all preserved the expected final admission state. This is **positive evidence of applied CPU competition**, not precise general-purpose performance benchmarking. The two runtimes have different process startup/runtime overhead, and shared-runner scheduling is noisy. A one-off ratio is not a system-wide throughput or resilience guarantee.

## Interpretation and remaining falsifiers

- **Supported within scope:** both in-memory synchronized prototypes maintain the four transaction invariants under these deterministic two-worker scenarios; a measured scheduling delay does not change final state; a stale-snapshot decision can violate capacity, spending cap and idempotency; a controlled one-core load visibly slowed both implementations without changing tested outcomes.
- **Not supported:** crash-consistent recovery, persistence guarantees, process interruption, real wallet debiting, dynamic resource discovery, multi-process coordination, distributed locking, livelock/starvation freedom or full-load capacity.
- **Method value:** deterministic barriers and an explicit stale-snapshot counterexample qualify the relevant concurrency invariant more reliably than unconstrained stress alone. CPU saturation adds load evidence but did not reveal additional business defects in this small experiment. Do **not** mandate all perturbations across projects.
- **Next cheapest discriminators:** durable reservation journal with induced process kill/restart and recovery invariants; targeted failure between prepare and commit; controlled duplicate/retry/reconciliation; comparison against a formally modeled transition specification. Only then consider disk or network faults when the architecture contains those dependencies.

**Method and language decisions remain DEFER.** No production site, booking system, provider account, CI monitor or global testing policy was altered.
