# Synthesis Review

## Verdict
PASS

## Criterion 1: Accurate representation
The synthesis faithfully represents the underlying reports. Every major claim traces back to specific report evidence: the RQ1 characterization of graph UI frameworks accurately reflects each report's findings (React Flow as "nodes are arbitrary React components," AntV X6 as "native virtual rendering," JointJS as "viewport virtualization in core with turnkey option in commercial tier," Cytoscape.js as "lacks native virtualization"). The RQ4 database claims — Neo4j's entity-state versioning pattern, Memgraph's in-memory advantage, Kuzu's archival (October 2025, Apple acqui-hire), PostgreSQL's ~100× recursive CTE penalty at 6 hops, SQLite's single-writer constraint — are all accurately drawn from their respective reports. The RQ3 conflict characterization (Temporal's "Beyond State Machines" positioning, Camunda's compensation semantics, LangGraph's checkpoint ownership, Burr's state-machine overlap with XState) matches the gap analyses. The RQ6 evidence chain (workflow provenance recovery, Mylyn field study, HCI cue research, Vispute & Kadam's reasoning-provenance distinction) is correctly attributed. The one area where representation is slightly simplified is the H5 verdict's characterization of all four RQ1 frameworks as supporting "low-to-moderate customization" — Cytoscape.js explicitly states "not without substantial customization" for rich content and virtualization — but this nuance is partially recovered in Finding 1's later paragraphs, which note Cytoscape.js requires "HTML-overlay extensions and ongoing canvas↔DOM synchronisation — not zero-effort." Overall, the synthesis is a fair and traceable representation of its inputs.

## Criterion 2: No invention
No claims in the synthesis were found that lack a basis in the underlying reports. The synthesis does not fabricate statistics, findings, or characterizations. Where the synthesis draws conclusions that go beyond what the reports directly state — such as the cross-cutting finding that "provenance is the novel contribution, not artefacts or lifecycles" — it correctly traces the reasoning to RQ7 Finding F4 (none of the five methodologies provides PROV-style provenance) and RQ6 Finding F3 (provenance is the load-bearing recovery substrate), both of which are directly supported by the respective reports. The synthesis does not introduce new concepts, tools, or benchmarks that are absent from the reports. Unsupported conclusions are consistently labelled as assumptions, interpretations, or low-confidence (see Criterion 4).

## Criterion 3: No omission of conflicting evidence
The synthesis identifies and represents the key disagreements and gaps present in the reports. It explicitly notes the recursive CTE performance tension in RQ4 — the ~100× vendor-adjacent figure versus the counter-argument that CTEs "solve 95% of graph problems" — and correctly characterises these as describing different scales and depths rather than true contradictions. The synthesis flagging that "no report directly contradicts another" is an accurate assessment of the corpus: the reports are gap analyses that identify tensions between EDASES's intent and existing tool assumptions, not studies that conflict with each other. The synthesis also surfaces the absence of evidence where relevant: no independent 5,000-node benchmark exists (flagged in H5 and the cross-cutting uncertainty section), no reversed engine/statechart composition is evidenced (flagged in Cross-cutting Finding 1), and direct LLM-agent validation of artefact-only recovery is absent (flagged in H2 and H3). The one notable nuance — that Cytoscape.js requires substantial rather than low-to-moderate customization — is partially addressed in H5 Finding 1, though not foregrounded in the hypothesis verdict itself.

## Criterion 4: Unsupported conclusions labelled
Unsupported conclusions are clearly and consistently labelled throughout the synthesis. The hypothesis assessments use explicit confidence levels (Medium, Medium-High, High) with stated justifications for each. The synthesis explicitly distinguishes what the reports support from what remains unvalidated: H1's verdict states "no report directly compares artefact-centric vs repository-centric architectures" and downgrades the confidence accordingly; H2's verdict notes the claim is "supported by analogy, not direct evidence" and references the designed-but-not-executed experiment; H3 and H4 are labelled Medium-High with the gap of direct agent-level validation explicitly noted. The "Remaining uncertainties" section systematically catalogues what is unknown (quantitative performance, reversed composition, artefact-only recovery, provenance granularity, eventual-consistency tolerance, EDASES lifecycle shape). Assumptions within hypothesis assessments — such as the event-sourcing vs immutable-version-nodes choice (U1 in RQ5), the XState→graph persistence wiring (U2 in RQ5), and the 5,000-actor feasibility (assumed in RQ2) — are flagged with "treated as an assumption" or "open design decision." The synthesis does not present any conclusion as established fact when the underlying report treats it as unverified.
