# Can resource owners witness one Attachment Point handover?

Suppose a continuing Work Unit W exposes two independently protected resources through the same Attachment Point P. X can append to either until one replacement R hands P to Y. If resource A returns a definitive stale-tenure denial to X and **after that reply** X initiates a fresh append at resource B which succeeds, R must precede the stale decision and follow the append. Each resource can exhibit a legal local handover. Together they contradict the single handover promised to W. Both calls can occur entirely during R. Waiting for both resources before acknowledging R does not repair the observed history.

This is a counterexample for a particular consumer contract. Both writes are otherwise admissible. A stale-tenure reply asserts that X's tenure ended. If A may return an uninformative busy result while X remains current, that reply supplies no such evidence. Neither the neutral requirements nor the three primitive names settle which reply an implementation promises. An experiment that only compares the final contents, or only sends probes after R returns, misses the distinction.

## A finite witness for a shared decision

Fix one completed replacement and a finite history of completed, fresh single-resource calls. Each terminal outcome in the test specifies one side of R. The protected data projection is a separate sequence of accepted payloads at each resource. A local witness orders the decisions at that resource and reproduces its sequence. A multi-resource read, a shared capacity limit or an observed global sequence would add constraints and falls outside this result. Record invocation and response as distinct ordered events. A sequential test client suffices to establish the crucial real-time edge. Accepting X requires a decision before R. Rejecting X for stale tenure requires a decision after R. Accepting the successor also requires a decision after R. The theorem concerns only these phase-determining operations. A transient busy response supplies no phase constraint when the contract permits it in either phase. Additional observed ordering through other operations would have to be retained if checking the full interface. All other data, rights and policy conditions are held fixed.

For each old-phase decision take its invocation. For each new-phase decision take its response. Let L be the latest of the old invocations and R's invocation. Let U be the earliest of the new responses and R's response. There is a common decision instant exactly when L < U, provided each resource's local history already realizes the stipulated before/after order and its content observations. Work in gaps between endpoint events. Necessity follows by putting the instant inside R, after every old invocation and before every new response. For sufficiency, put it in the open interval (L,U). At each resource use its legal local operation order. Its old-phase prefix fits before the cut and its new-phase suffix fits after it. For a finite ordered list of calls with intervals, instants can be assigned in order arbitrarily close after the running maximum of their invocations. Local feasibility ensures this maximum is below each response. Apply this construction to each prefix and suffix with the chosen cut as the extra upper or lower bound. Merge the local schedules by assigned instant. Real-time precedence is preserved, and any equal instants on independent resources can be separated in the same gap.

A failed intersection has two extremal witnesses, possibly one boundary of R. Once local adequacy holds, at most two resource observations plus R refute the common cut, however many resources or calls the history contains. This is a witness-size bound on recorded histories. A faulty program can wait for its thousandth resource to join before producing the first such history. Testing a two-resource deployment gives no program-size cutoff.

The restriction to a single shared change matters. For each n ≥ 3, take n initially clear bits. Concurrent request i reads bit i+1 modulo n as clear and sets bit i. If all succeed, their required order is one directed cycle of length n. Drop any one request and the order becomes a path with a legal serial execution. No fixed bound on the number of requests witnesses all such failures. This family extends the historical three-request ordering challenge. It explains why a small-witness argument must name the shape of the dependency it covers. ## A construction for repeated replacements

Consider a fixed, nonempty finite set of gates covering every path by which P can append. A gate is the authority at the resource that orders both the admission decision and the complete append. One durable selector for W/P serializes authorized replacement decisions, with a non-reused generation e. The old and successor requests carry authenticated provenance for their issuing W/P and tenure. The selector can acknowledge replacement as soon as its decision is durable. Delivery of control notices and votes may be delayed, reordered or repeated.

| Event | Authorized transition |
| --- | --- |
| Begin replacement | The selector initiates the move from e to e+1. At most one undecided move exists for P. A new move may begin while activation notices for a committed move are still in transit. |
| Prepare a gate | An open gate at h acts only on an authentic prepare for h+1. It orders the freeze against all local appends, durably closes admission, then issues a vote bound to W/P, gate identity, membership configuration and h+1. Previously accepted bytes remain available for continuation. |
| Commit replacement | The selector verifies one matching vote from each distinct required member and atomically records e+1 as current. A duplicate can satisfy only its own member's entry. This decision is the abstract replacement point. The successor can now obtain its grant. |
| Activate a gate | A frozen gate at h opens for h+1 only on an authentic committed decision for that exact successor. Other commands can be ignored or retransmitted. |
| Deliver a fresh request | An open gate for h accepts an otherwise permitted request from h and appends the payload as one local event. It returns stale for an earlier grant. A frozen gate defers both append and definitive tenure replies. A waiting request is checked again when it can resolve. |

**Inductive argument.** Select any member i and let h be its last installed generation. When open, h equals the selector's current e. When frozen, either h=e with the move to e+1 under way, or h+1=e and the activation notice can still be in transit. A recorded vote credited to the current move implies that i is frozen at e. Initially every member is open at generation zero. Freezing establishes the first frozen case. An authentic vote can arrive long after it was sent, but while its target is still the undecided successor, the signer cannot yet have reopened. A commit includes i's vote, so the commit takes i from the first frozen case into the second. An activation moves it into the open case. Steps private to another member preserve this reasoning. Beginning the next replacement leaves a lagging member frozen until it catches up.

Universal generalization over i gives the invariant for any fixed finite membership and induction over steps covers arbitrarily many replacements. Project the concrete state onto (W, P, selected tenure, per-resource accepted sequences). Prepare, vote and activate leave this projection unchanged. Commit changes only the selected tenure. The open-gate invariant makes every definitive append or stale reply a legal reference decision at the local point. This proves finite-prefix safety of the abstract protocol given the specified local events. Whether a candidate has such an event, retains the actual accepted bytes after executor loss, or covers every alias requires separate evidence.

**Where the trust moved.** An append needs no synchronous read of the selector. A coordinator that only collects votes need not be trusted to judge whether every member froze. The selector can check the complete configured set of generation-bound votes itself. Authority issuance, member enumeration, truth of each durable freeze before its vote escapes, a unique current selector across recovery, and local whole-effect mediation remain trusted. A signature authenticates a vote's source. It cannot establish that the signer fenced a buffered append or that an omitted resource has no writable alias. The experiment models authentic provenance at this interface and implements no cryptography.

**Availability cost.** Assume a disconnected open gate can still serve X locally, an idle current X must be allowed to append, and the selector can finish a replacement without any evidence that the gate stopped. Compare two silent prefixes at that gate. In one, X remains current. In the other, the selector has acknowledged Y before X invokes an append. The gate sees the same state, request and messages, but a definitive answer must differ. A protocol with those observations must give up either the local service guarantee or the disconnected replacement guarantee. A prior irrevocable fence or an expiry contract with trustworthy timing changes the observation premise. The construction above waits for the freeze. Failure of a member can therefore block replacement indefinitely. Gray and Lamport's treatment of preparation and blocking gives the relevant commit-protocol comparison. This construction has a fixed membership, one decision authority, and no abort or failover protocol. [Gray and Lamport, *Consensus on Transaction Commit*](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0408036)

The bounded schedule search separately supplies reachable witnesses. |

The symbolic check adds a ghost integer v, the greatest generation voted for by the chosen gate. At an open gate v=h. At a frozen gate v=h+1. It permits receipt of **any** vote generation in [1,v], and of commands from any initiated or committed generation as appropriate. Thus the receipt step can use messages from elsewhere in transit without consulting the live gate. The concrete-to-symbolic obligation is that every authentic delivered message lies in its indicated prefix and has the correct scope. Suppression or duplication is harmless to this safety invariant. The bounded model omits stuttering deliveries and checks useful evolution, including eventual activation *on some schedule*. The two solvers reduce dependence on one implementation of arithmetic reasoning. They share the formula and cannot detect an incorrectly chosen interface. ## What this establishes for ASES

* **Demonstrated.** A common Work Unit handover can fail observably even when disjoint resources each pass their local replacement test. A definitive stale reply followed causally by a fresh successful X call is a portable falsifier. A test driver can establish that edge by issuing the second call only after receiving the first reply. Cross-host wall-clock comparison is unnecessary. If calls overlap, reply order alone does not establish a violation.
* **Conditional.** The interval theorem gives a complete handover-placement test for the stated history fragment. The arbitrary-member invariant gives a sufficient construction for repeated replacements. ISOLATE must protect the selector, gates and accepted contents. CONNECT must bind genuine scoped requests and votes. ORDER must relate each whole local append to its freeze and each activation to one committed replacement. Under these contracts the three responsibilities can realize this slice without a distributed transaction on every append.
* **Hypothesis.** A candidate owner could expose these events, or implement the same contract by directly serializing every decision at one owner. If the consumer allows separately visible handovers per resource, the two-call witness instead distinguishes that weaker interface. Which guarantee a real W/P advertises is a design decision.
* **Remaining proof obligations.** For a candidate implementation, map W/P and both tenures to independently authenticated owner evidence. Enumerate writable aliases, prove the local append/freeze boundary, then exercise both W6 orders and the two-call cross-resource schedule. Check retained payloads at Y and distinguish fresh delayed writes from replay of an accepted outcome. Dynamic membership requires ordering join or removal with the decision's voter set. Delegation, multi-resource atomic effects, incompatible recovered snapshots, external sinks and progress under failure each require their own refinement. General kernel adequacy, irreducibility of the triad and end-to-end Work Unit conformance remain open.
