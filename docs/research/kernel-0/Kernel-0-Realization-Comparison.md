# Kernel-0 Bounded Realization Comparison

## Fixed contract and comparison method

This comparison holds the [abstract semantics](./Kernel-0-Abstract-Semantics.md) and [verification obligations](./Kernel-0-Verification-Obligations.md) fixed. The first experiment protects authoritative work and position data, current authority, and every mutable live dependency of accepted meaning. It covers executor loss, including loss of authority data co-located with that executor. It does not claim independent authority-service restart, arbitrary external actions, exactly-once retry, or eventual scheduling. Requests requiring different outcomes after replacement must carry non-confusable trusted evidence; physical old-producer exclusion after it obtains new authority adds source binding. Interacting commitments must admit one acyclic order with coherent current validation views. This is a research comparison, not a technology selection or implementation instruction.

The criterion for a route is a concrete correspondence from its records, requests, protected effects, and failure traces to that contract. A route is not simpler when it delegates a necessary guarantee to an unnamed store, operating system, caller, bus, or external service. A proof of code alone cannot establish the authenticity of authority evidence, the truth of supplied policy, persistence across the claimed loss, or atomicity at an external effect sink.

## Cross-cutting architecture: immutable candidates and a small acceptance boundary

The smallest plausible *shape* is to let executors produce candidate material outside the authoritative boundary, then commit a guarded acceptance together with current authority and continuity changes in one coherent view. For the first finite experiment, acceptance can incorporate the required candidate value into the authority holder's retained state. A reference to external bytes is equivalent only when their integrity, availability, and retention through the claimed executor-loss boundary are trusted; a hash alone cannot restore bytes destroyed with an executor. Candidate production may be speculative, but accepted content needed for continuation cannot be lost or mutated through an unguarded path. This narrows the protected surface without assuming a general transaction system for all candidate work. It does not guarantee candidate correctness or make a live mutable referent safe. The shape can be realized in any family below.

## Realization families

| Family | Trusted boundary and semantic correspondence | Persistence, order, and external effects | Proof burden and experimental fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Still-live in-memory authority service | One isolated service owns the *entire* authoritative view, retained accepted content, and guarded transition. Its application code, compiler/runtime, OS isolation, and authority-evidence ingress are trusted. Executors cannot write its state directly. A serial event loop or service-local lock can order whole transitions if no bypass path exists. | The state need only outlive executors for the fixed profile; service restart is outside it. A tentative successor must not be visible until the complete state is published as the commitment. An executor dying mid-request can leave a pending/unknown caller outcome, but cannot expose partial authoritative mutation. A protected external sink still needs a separate contract. | Smallest apparent first falsification vehicle. Its simplicity is conditional on one still-live service holding all relevant state, including accepted content; it offers no independent service-failure assurance. This is a deduction from the fixed semantics, not a documented platform guarantee. |
| Transactional conventional software | Application guard/effect code, compiler/runtime, OS and isolation, authority-evidence issuer/validator, and transactional store are trusted. The app must map the entire guard and whole effect to one actual store commit; a prior read or process-local mutex among independent writers is insufficient. | A transactional store can supply a candidate atomic/serializable commit, but the mapping must also preserve completed-before-initiated precedence for affected operations across application, store, and any cache or replica. Storage configuration and live dependencies remain trusted. A protected external sink needs its own contract. | A second progressive route when independent authority-process restart, multi-writer placement, or persistent storage becomes an explicit requirement. Runtime tests show counterexamples but do not prove conformance. SQLite documents serializable writes and atomic transaction recovery as available *mechanisms*, not certification of this application or stack. [Isolation](https://www.sqlite.org/isolation.html), [atomic commit](https://www.sqlite.org/atomiccommit.html). |
| Source-verified code with ordinary compilation (for example Verus) | Source-level implementation and invariants can be proved under declared trusted specifications. Compiler, runtime, OS, storage, external calls, and assumptions at unverified boundaries remain trusted; the verified source proof must be connected to actual transaction behavior. | Persistence and cross-component atomicity are not supplied by the source verifier. An external call specified as atomic is an assumption until its implementation is checked. | Valuable for a small guard/transition proof after the model is fixed; it does not by itself reduce the whole-system trusted boundary. Verus explicitly documents assumption mechanisms and says its aim is not compiler verification. [Verus assumptions](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/tcb.html), [Verus guide](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/). |
| Verified compiler path (for example CakeML/Pancake or CompCert) | A proved program-to-machine-code relation can remove much compiler trust for supported source and targets. It still needs a proof of the Kernel-0 program, its I/O semantics, trusted runtime/OS or bare-metal interface, storage and evidence ingress. | Verified compilation does not make a storage transaction, reset recovery, or external sink atomic. These must be modeled and joined to the compiled program's behavior. | Serious later assurance route when compiler trust is material. Considerable integration and proof work before it tests the tiny substrate hypothesis. CompCert states a machine-checked source-to-code guarantee; CakeML describes its verified backend and proof-producing translation. [CompCert](https://compcert.org/), [CakeML](https://cakeml.org/). |
| Exact code or ISA-level route (for example Bedrock2/RISC-V) | Prove the actual instruction sequence or a connected low-level compilation path against the abstract transition. A formal ISA model helps define instruction behavior; processor, memory model, ABI, boot/firmware, drivers, device I/O, and any OS boundary still need explicit treatment. A verified OS can narrow some OS trust but does not verify the application or persistent effects. | Concrete atomic instructions or memory fences alone do not make multi-record persistent commits or external actions atomic. Reset and device traces must be in the correspondence argument. | Highest near-term proof/integration cost for this small hypothesis, though potentially useful for a later tightly bounded trusted component. Bedrock2 is explicitly work in progress; the official RISC-V Sail model is an ISA specification, not a storage proof. [Bedrock2](https://github.com/mit-plv/bedrock2), [Sail RISC-V](https://github.com/riscv/sail-riscv), [seL4 proof scope](https://sel4.systems/Verification/proofs.html). |
| Processorless logic/RTL/FPGA | A circuit can make a small guard and state transition directly visible and can be model checked or compared with a netlist. Trust moves to RTL semantics, synthesis/place-and-route/bitstream, physical device, clocks/reset, I/O interface, and state holder. Calling it processorless does not remove those assumptions. | A purely volatile circuit cannot by itself preserve authority and continuity across loss of the circuit's state. The fixed executor-loss profile can use a still-live circuit, but any stronger power/restart claim needs trusted nonvolatile storage and recovery. Protected external effects still require a trusted effect sink. | Serious alternative for a very small transition engine when direct hardware enforcement is the question; poor first test of the Work Unit hypothesis if interface and durable-store engineering dominates. YosysHQ documents formal property and equivalence tools; FPGA vendor documentation illustrates volatile configuration on an SRAM-based family. [YosysHQ tools](https://yosyshq.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools.html), [Yosys model checking](https://yosyshq.readthedocs.io/projects/yosys/en/latest/using_yosys/more_scripting/model_checking.html), [AMD Virtex-6 configuration](https://docs.amd.com/api/khub/documents/lQIMkz9EbNKvrHsjwRDEpA/content). |

These are families, not a ranking by prestige. A verified compiler route and an exact-code route can overlap; source verification plus a verified compiler is stronger than either label alone only when their proof boundaries compose. A transactional store can also be used beneath verified code or RTL. A single live authority service may serialize unrelated commitments as an implementation choice; the semantics does not require that global order, and concurrent executors can still submit compatible work. The number of named components is less informative than which components are actually trusted for the guarded commitment.

## Criteria tested against the fixed semantics

| Obligation | Discriminating realization question |
| --- | --- |
| Current guarded whole commitment | Does the same concrete atomic boundary cover authority validity, all invariant dependencies, and the whole accepted effect? |
| Coherent interacting order | Can three-way guard cycles and concurrent invariant conflicts be ruled out across *all* paths? Does the concrete order also put an acknowledged relevant change before an affected request initiated afterward, including through caches or replicas? |
| Non-confusable replacement | Who issues and authenticates current evidence, and can an old producer copy, replay, or acquire the replacement's evidence? |
| Executor-loss continuity | Where do the continuing work, position, authority, and accepted content live while no executor is running? What happens if the executor dies mid-commit or destroys the only bytes behind an accepted reference? |
| Protected live dependencies | Can an accepted result still change through a mutable location that bypasses the guard? If so, which component mediates it? |
| Concrete-to-abstract correspondence | Which implementation events count as commit, deny, and pending, and which concrete observations map to one `σ`? |
| Additional failure profiles | If independent service or storage interruption is later claimed, what durable record and recovery order restore an allowed abstract state? |

## Provisional judgment and unresolved alternatives

The still-live in-memory service with guarded whole acceptance is the least elaborate **first falsification vehicle** under the fixed executor-loss profile. It can keep all necessary work, position, authority, and accepted candidate content in one state holder while independently terminating executors. This is an inference from the fixed contract, not a proof that an implementation conforms. Its software, isolation, evidence-ingress, and service-availability assumptions must be stated. It cannot support an independent service-restart claim; the transactional-store route is a separate progressive experiment when that stronger failure or placement need is established. A store's generic serializability does not by itself prove the abstract completed-before-initiated precedence: a revocation acknowledged before an old request starts must precede that request, even if another serialization could explain their raw operations.

For later assurance, a verified source/program route and a small RTL transition engine remain live alternatives. Their relative cost depends on the exact I/O and persistence boundary. A verified compiler may reduce compiler assumptions without reducing storage or device assumptions; a circuit may reduce runtime assumptions while introducing synthesis, physical, and persistent-interface assumptions. Exact ISA-level work is a possible proof target, but the present semantics does not force it. No route has yet demonstrated an end-to-end refinement or a smaller *total* trusted boundary for this profile.

The next discriminating work is a formal finite model of the fixed semantics and Work Unit witness, followed by a tiny still-live authority-service experiment only if the model's non-vacuity and adversarial traces hold. The service should own one complete authoritative state with two positions and the accepted content needed for continuation. Probe replacement with distinguishable authority evidence, conflicting independent writes, a three-request cycle, executor death before and after commitment, and the completed-revocation/begun-later-old-request trace. Attempt a mutable-live-reference bypass and loss of the sole external candidate copy. A safe experiment either retains accepted content in the service or supplies a declared trusted immutable/available holder before acceptance. It need not choose a production language, database, or circuit technology. The experiment must record concrete trusted assumptions and a trace-to-model mapping before its result can support a realization claim.

**WHY:** The retained semantic contract determines the necessary ordering, evidence, continuity, and effect boundaries; each family moves rather than erases some of those obligations.

**WHAT:** The provisional abstract semantics and verification target, the finite Work Unit witness, one independent Astra architecture challenge, and the linked primary tool/project documentation for available mechanisms and their limits.

**HOW CERTAIN:** Evidence-based comparison of proof and trusted-boundary structure; the first-experiment preference is an inference. No family's conformance is established.

**WHAT-NOT-TESTED:** No implementation, circuit, formal model, proof composition, storage-failure experiment, authority-evidence mechanism, or external sink was built or checked. Independent substrate restart and stronger external-action profiles were not assessed end to end.
