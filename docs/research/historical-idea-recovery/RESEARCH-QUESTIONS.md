## R24 — reviewer independence versus evidence completeness

A historical adversarial review episode reportedly yielded a decisive falsification from the reviewer with full context, while less-informed reviewers returned partial verdicts. This is an observation, not a controlled causal result. One-shot independence and evidence completeness are separate variables: a fresh reviewer can receive sufficient relevant source evidence without receiving the builder's conclusions or answer labels. Compare matched fresh-review arms with differing evidence availability, including valid defect detection, cross-document contradictions, false alarms, time and token cost. A separate session-history comparison would require its own controls. This complements, rather than duplicates, graph-versus-source representation and review-gate-value questions. The framework comparison should measure construction separately from authoring and deployment security/operational surfaces; the remote-execution question concerns independently revocable authority, not trusting a local model or recalling data already sent to a provider.
