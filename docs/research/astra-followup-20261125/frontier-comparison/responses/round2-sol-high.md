# Kernel-0 should be a guarded, durable commit boundary

The smallest candidate I can defend has **stable protected addresses, revocable authority, and one atomic conditional commit**. A commit can change ordinary state, install a grant, or clear a grant. A grant can depend on an exact earlier grant, so replacing or revoking that earlier grant also disables its descendants. Work Units and Attachment Points are arrangements of these primitives. An Execution exercises authority through a replaceable binding.

The essential guarantee is that **every authoritative mutation has a valid basis at the point it takes effect**, including mutations that confer authority on someone else. The evidence establishes that requirement, durable continuity, and exclusivity where configured. It leaves room for different choices about delegation, conflict granularity, and where authority ends when an operation leaves ASES. I used the pinned packet as the sole source. ## 1. Ontology and transition semantics

| Distinction | Candidate representation |
|---|---|
| Something protected persists while its contents change | A canonical address with a revision and a record. Addresses have a fixed kind, either data or grant slot. |
| A request has an authenticated origin | A subject established at ingress. Authentication identifies the requester. A current grant authorizes the action. |
| Permission can survive or cease independently of the requester | A grant binding with a holder, exact rights to use, exact rights to pass on, and a reference to its parent grant incarnation. A slot has at most one current binding. |
| The outcome of a request can depend on intervening events | A request supplies expected revisions for its writes and any state it relies on. The kernel validates the request against one authoritative state and applies the whole change or leaves that state intact. |

A grant incarnation is a slot address plus its revision. Clearing a slot advances its revision and retains enough information to prevent reuse. Reissuing the same grant to the same holder still creates a new incarnation. Durable data can use the same versioned store. Data payloads are inert to the kernel. A sentence in a work record cannot grant permission.

A right names a fixed operation and a canonical target, such as editing data at `k` or managing grant slot `s`. Exact rights suffice for the candidate. The initial state supplies a trusted authority root from which later grants derive. Namespace-wide rights, expiry, and richer policy expressions can be evaluated separately.

The only mutation entry point is `Commit(expected, changes, proofs)`. Its change vocabulary is closed.

- **`Put(k, value)`** sets a data value or tombstone. It requires a current grant to the requester for editing `k`.
- **`Bind(s, holder, use, pass, parent)`** installs a fresh binding. It requires current authority to manage `s` and a live parent binding held by the requester. Every right in `use` or `pass` must occur in the parent’s pass set. Managing a slot and supplying the rights to install in it are distinct checks.
- **`Clear(s)`** empties a slot and requires current authority to manage it. The new revision invalidates the previous binding and every grant that depends on it. `Bind` and `Clear` can share one slot-update operation with occupied and empty cases.

A binding is live when it is the current incarnation of its slot and every binding in its parent chain is live back to a trusted root. A commit checks the expected revisions, the rights for every change, and the liveness of all proofs against the same pre-state. Each newly installed grant must also have a live parent in the resulting state. Parents come from the pre-state, so a grant can only cite an earlier incarnation and a request cannot manufacture permission for itself. A valid commit becomes one durable change. A rejected request leaves authoritative state intact.

This gives revocation a precise ordering. An operation admitted before revocation can commit. If revocation takes effect first, the same proof fails. A batch can use a right and clear its own grant in the same atomic step, under the stated pre-state rule. A new grant whose parent that batch clears is rejected. Those rules are sufficient and explicit. A deployment could choose to forbid use and revocation in the same batch.

For an **exclusive resource**, the candidate permits one fixed additional authorization rule. A canonical operation on that resource must be authorized directly by the current binding of its designated slot. A descendant grant or an independently issued grant cannot satisfy that rule. The slot manager can transfer the right by rebinding the slot. If several names reach the same underlying resource, the rule must cover the underlying operation through every alias. Where observation itself is protected, reads need equivalent mediation. Neither clearing a grant nor replacing its holder can retract information already disclosed.

## 2. How the Work Unit architecture composes

Take a Work Unit `w` with durable data under its stable identity. Give each Attachment Point `a` a stable identity under `w`, a separate continuity record, and a designated execution grant slot. The relationship can be structural in the addresses. The records and slot have no resident process.

- **Attach and replace.** A controller binds the slot to a fresh execution subject with the required rights. Replacing the binding fences the old subject and all authority derived from that binding. The Work Unit and the Attachment Point retain their identities and durable state. If the handoff must adopt a particular checkpoint, the replacement includes the checkpoint revision as a precondition. That gives a racing checkpoint write and a handoff a definite order.
- **Continue after loss.** The replacement reads the durable continuity record and current Work Unit state. A dead executor can take its uncommitted computation with it. Accepted changes and their recoverable state survive independently.
- **Share or isolate.** Two Attachment Points can have rights to the same Work Unit address, rights to disjoint addresses, or a mix. A shared document can admit either grant while a protected publication operation admits only the holder of its designated slot. The same transition rules cover one executor, successive executors, and concurrent executors.
- **Resolve conflicts.** If two executors submit changes based on revision 7 of a shared record, the first accepted write advances the revision and the second proposal fails its guard. The second executor can reread and form a new proposal. A change derived from several records supplies all relevant revisions. A predicate such as “no other attachment exists” also needs a protected directory or other canonical address that changes on insertion. Guarding the records already observed misses concurrent insertions.

The fencing argument depends on the issuance configuration. Give each run a fresh execution identity and route **every** authority path available to that identity through its attachment binding. A separately rooted grant issued to the same identity survives replacement. If ASES must support independent issuers while guaranteeing attachment-wide revocation, make the exact attachment incarnation a mandatory dependency of every grant usable in that execution context, and carry that dependency through delegation. Checking it only at login would let a helper keep exercising derived authority after the run ends. This stronger interpretation requires a second dependency in some grants. The basic Work Unit construction uses one.

Consider a run that delegates a narrow edit right to a helper and then loses its attachment. If the helper’s edit commits first, the replacement sees that edit in durable state. If replacement commits first, the helper’s parent chain is dead and the edit fails even with a fresh data revision. If the old process later reconnects, its old credential cannot restore the replaced grant incarnation. This trace exercises continuity, indirect revocation, and the difference between a data conflict and a loss of authority.

## 3. What survives attempts at elimination

| Attempted reduction | Counterexample and what it establishes |
|---|---|
| Identify work with the executor or its current state value | A process dies and its successor must resume the same work at the same Attachment Point. Two work items with identical contents can have different authority. Some stable identity and durable state are necessary. A tree of built-in kernel objects is optional. |
| Authenticate the caller and dispense with current grants | A recognized worker is reassigned while its old session remains alive. Its identity still authenticates. Permission must be a separately enforced, current relation. An ACL, capability, or equivalent representation can supply it. |
| Keep only a slot name or compare the grant’s contents | A slot is cleared and later reinstated with the same holder and rights. An old request or child grant appears valid again. Revocable reuse needs an incarnation distinction or an equivalent guarantee against resurrection. A per-slot integer is one representation. |
| Represent grants as ordinary writable records | A worker with checkpoint access writes a record declaring itself the publisher. If that record confers authority, checkpoint access escalates privilege. One store and one commit API can serve both kinds of state. Their validation rules must differ. |
| Let use imply the ability to pass on a right | A temporary reviewer forwards its access to another subject. If authority to exercise a right can be granted without the power to redistribute it, the substrate must preserve this distinction. A separate pass set suffices. A governor-only model can dispense with general delegation. |
| Copy derived rights and discard their source | A run grants a helper access and is then superseded. The helper holds an apparently valid independent copy. Revocation that follows delegation requires a dependency or equivalent atomic invalidation. Parent references can be eliminated when all grants are independent and dependent delegation is outside the contract. |
| Check authority first and apply changes later | Revocation lands between the check and the write. A two-part request can also write its first part before discovering the second is unauthorized. Authorization and mutation need a common atomic decision for each claimed authoritative change. Global serialization and arbitrary multi-address batches are sufficient choices. A smaller state layout can use coarser records or coordination limited to conflicting operations. |
| Order writes but omit checks on what they were based on | Two approvals each reserve the last available place after reading the same count, and both update different records. A total write order alone preserves neither approval’s premise. The substrate needs a way to test relevant preconditions when a guarantee depends on them. Per-address revision equality is sufficient. Global compare-and-swap is smaller to specify and rejects unrelated intervening changes. |
| Use an exclusive slot as a convention while ordinary grants still reach the resource | A second grant edits the same protected resource through the ordinary path. Exclusivity requires every applicable path to respect a common constraint. The direct-slot rule can be dropped if issuance itself structurally guarantees there is only one possible live holder for that operation. |
| Add a kernel verb for each lifecycle or workflow action | Attaching, handing off, suspending, and resuming can all be expressed through data updates and binding changes. Queueing, scheduling, review outcomes, merge policy, and role names can remain above this boundary. A clock is needed only if authority depends on time, which requires an explicit time model. |

The lower bound here is on the **distinctions a correct system must preserve**. It does not establish a unique vocabulary or representation. In particular, the packet leaves open whether executors need delegated authority at all. A governor-only candidate with direct revocable bindings is smaller if each indirect action is freshly admitted using the original subject and its current rights. The lineage candidate earns its extra rule when a delegate must act in its own right and still lose authority when its source is revoked.

## 4. Indirect authority and composition

**Power follows the operation a component can cause.** If a worker can write a branch name and a controller automatically publishes whatever branch is named, that write can exercise publication authority indirectly. The controller can either submit the exact publication operation under the worker’s currently valid rights, or make its own authorized publication decision. In the first case, the request identifies the original subject, action, canonical target, and relevant versions. In the second, the controller owns the decision. A queue entry carries intent or evidence. It cannot silently convert the enqueue-time authority check into a durable permission to publish.

The candidate permits multiple grants held by the same requester to authorize different changes in one batch. Each change needs its own complete proof. Each new binding must derive from one parent with sufficient pass rights. In particular, one grant’s edit right and another grant’s power to manage a slot do not make the edit right transferable. If an operation genuinely needs approval from two independent subjects, this is a different guarantee. One sufficient extension is explicit consent from each subject bound to the same operation, target, parameters, and relevant revisions, with the applicable freshness rules checked at admission. Two ordinary grants held by one controller establish a different fact.

Revocation follows declared dependencies. Revoking a grant used to **manage** an installation removes future management authority. It does not automatically invalidate rights that were validly installed from a separate parent. If an installation must remain contingent on both the manager’s authority and the supplying grant, both must be represented as continuing dependencies. Likewise, an independent grant can still authorize the same operation after one grant is revoked. Revoking a grant, removing a subject’s access, and closing a resource to a class of operations are distinct policies.

## 5. Recovery and the external boundary

**Local recovery.** The abstract state recovers to a committed prefix that includes every acknowledged commit and preserves revision non-reuse. A lost reply can leave the requester unsure whether its commit landed. Retrying the identical guarded batch cannot apply the same writes twice, because the first success advances their revisions. That alone does not tell the requester who made an intervening write, or whether its earlier commit landed and was later superseded. Where exact outcome attribution matters, the contract needs a durable decision keyed to the request, recorded in the same commit and protected against alteration. Restoring an old backup as current can resurrect authority and discard accepted work. The candidate’s recovery guarantee is conditional on durable, non-rollback state within a declared failure model.

**External effects.** A guarded commit can durably admit an exact intention, such as publishing a named artifact with a particular digest. It cannot establish that a remote service acted. Model admission, possible dispatch, and observed outcome as distinguishable events. A dispatch report or receipt is an explicit input with a named source and a defined meaning. A timeout leaves an unresolved outcome. If the action was admitted before revocation, the contract must specify whether dispatch may follow that admission or requires another current check. A check before sending still leaves a gap before a remote service acts. Enforcement at remote acceptance requires cooperation at that boundary, such as a recipient that rejects stale fencing tokens.

There is a concrete impossibility here. After a crash, “the request was never delivered” and “the service acted and the reply was lost” can leave identical durable local state. Retrying may duplicate the second effect. Declining to retry may omit the first. For a recipient with neither deduplication nor an authoritative way to discover the result, **guaranteed completion and guaranteed absence of duplicates cannot both hold**. Preserve the unresolved outcome. An idempotency key helps only if the recipient enforces it for the effect, with a suitable retention and failure contract.

I found no contradiction among the packet’s requirements under a substrate-local reading. Stronger versions do conflict. Immediate revocation and continued admission on a partitioned side that cannot learn of the revocation are incompatible. Durable recovery after executor loss is achievable. Recovery after loss or rollback of every authoritative copy requires a surviving source of truth. Resource isolation covers real effects only to the extent that all relevant paths are mediated. These limits belong in the verification claim. ## 6. What to settle before calling the model minimal

1. **Where does a right take effect?** Decide separately for local mutation, release of protected information, dispatch, and remote acceptance. This is the most consequential boundary. It determines which resources Kernel-0 can credibly claim to govern.
2. **What must revocation follow?** Decide whether revocation cuts one lineage, all activity attributable to an Execution, or all uses of a resource. Test an old Execution with a separately issued grant, a queued operation, and a delegate that outlives it. The answers determine whether direct bindings, single-parent lineage, or conjunctive dependencies are needed.
3. **Which invariants require a shared decision?** Identify actual exclusive resources and cross-record invariants. Specify what competing operations must observe in both orders. Disjoint operations can proceed independently when their data, guards, and authority dependencies do not interact. Global ordering is a useful specification choice, with finer coordination justified by the invariants.
4. **What survives each failure, and who can reestablish authority?** Executor loss, kernel crash, storage rollback, lost administrator credentials, and remote uncertainty are different cases. Decide which are in scope before claiming recoverability or specifying emergency authority.

The first verification target should cover authorization at commit, atomic rejection, attenuation, absence of stale-grant resurrection, exclusivity across all canonical access paths, and continuity across execution replacement and in-scope crashes. The model needs to represent both orders of revocation and use, conflicting commits, lost acknowledgment, and the indistinguishable remote outcomes. Any realization must separately establish authenticated ingress, complete mediation, atomic durable commits, and the stated recovery and recipient contracts. Those are concrete obligations at the boundary of the model.
